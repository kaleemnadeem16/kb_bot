## What AI is

Artificial intelligence is the practice of building software that performs tasks we normally associate with human thinking, such as recognising a face, translating a sentence or planning a route. Most modern systems get there by learning patterns from large amounts of data instead of following hand-written rules.

## Main areas

### Machine learning
Machine learning covers methods where a program improves at a task by studying examples. Instead of coding the rules for spotting spam, you show the model many spam and non-spam messages and let it work out the difference.

### Deep learning
Deep learning is a kind of machine learning built on neural networks with many stacked layers. It powers most current progress in image recognition, speech recognition and language models, but it usually needs a lot of data and computing power.

### Natural language processing
Natural language processing (NLP) is about getting computers to work with human language: reading, summarising, translating and answering questions about text.

### Computer vision
Computer vision teaches machines to make sense of images and video. Typical jobs are detecting objects, reading text from photos and spotting defects on a production line.

## Where AI is used

1. **Healthcare**: helping read medical scans, suggesting candidate drugs and supporting treatment planning.
2. **Transportation**: driver-assistance features, route optimisation and traffic prediction.
3. **Finance**: flagging suspicious transactions and estimating credit risk.
4. **Entertainment**: recommending films, music and games.
5. **Education**: adaptive exercises and automated feedback for learners.

## Looking ahead

AI systems will keep getting more capable, so questions about fairness, privacy, accuracy and accountability matter as much as the technology itself. Teams that deploy AI should test it on real data and keep a person responsible for important decisions.
